The problemالمشكلة

A colleague sends you the rent history of forty flats: rent_history_wide.csv, one row per listing and one column per quarter. You want the obvious thing — “how much did rents move from quarter to quarter?” — and there is no column to group by, no column to plot against, and no column a model could read as time. The quarters are not in the table; they are the table's column names. Every operation you know how to do needs the data in a different shape, and nothing in the file is wrong.

يرسل إليك زميلٌ تاريخَ إيجار أربعين شقة: rent_history_wide.csv، صفٌّ لكل إعلان وعمودٌ لكل ربع سنة. وتريد الشيء البديهي — «كم تحرّكت الإيجارات من ربع إلى ربع؟» — فلا تجد عمودًا تجمّع عليه ولا عمودًا ترسم مقابله ولا عمودًا يقرؤه النموذج زمنًا. فالأرباع ليست في الجدول؛ بل هي أسماء أعمدته. وكل عملية تعرف كيف تؤدّيها تحتاج البيانات في شكل آخر، ولا شيء في الملف خاطئ.

Tidy data, in one sentenceالبيانات المرتَّبة في جملة واحدة

A table is tidy when each variable is a column, each observation is a row, and each kind of observation gets its own table. That is the whole definition, and the only hard part is the second clause, because what counts as one observation depends on the question. For the chapter's question — “what rent should this flat ask?” — one observation is one flat, and the 480-flat frame of Lesson 3 is tidy. For the question “how did rents move over the year?” one observation is one flat in one quarter, and the file below is not tidy at all: a single observation is spread across four columns, and the variable called quarter exists only in the header.

يكون الجدول مرتَّبًا (tidy) حين يكون كل متغيّر عمودًا وكل مشاهدة صفًّا وكل نوع من المشاهدات جدولًا خاصًّا به. هذا هو التعريف كله، والجزء الصعب الوحيد هو الشرط الثاني، لأن ما يُعَدّ مشاهدةً واحدة يتبع السؤال. فبالنسبة لسؤال الفصل — «كم ينبغي أن تطلب هذه الشقة إيجارًا؟» — المشاهدة الواحدة شقةٌ واحدة، وإطارُ الشقق الـ480 في الدرس 3 مرتَّب. وبالنسبة لسؤال «كيف تحرّكت الإيجارات خلال السنة؟» المشاهدة الواحدة شقةٌ واحدة في ربع واحد، والملف أدناه ليس مرتَّبًا على الإطلاق: فالمشاهدة الواحدة موزّعة على أربعة أعمدة، والمتغيّر المسمّى الربع لا يوجد إلا في الترويسة.

The numbers survive the round trip; the table does not, quite. Three things changed and all three are worth knowing. listing_id is now the index rather than a column, so df["listing_id"] raises. city is gone, because it was neither the index nor the columns nor the values. And rent_2026q1 came back as float64 although the file held int64: melting put it in one column alongside quarters that have blanks, and a column with a blank cannot be an integer. The last one is Lesson 3's Int64 argument arriving by a new route, and it is why equals above needed an astype(float) to agree.

تنجو الأرقام من الرحلة؛ أما الجدول فلا، ليس تمامًا. تغيّرت ثلاثة أمور وكلها تستحق المعرفة. فصار listing_id فهرسًا لا عمودًا، فيرفع df["listing_id"] خطأً. واختفى city، لأنه لم يكن الفهرسَ ولا الأعمدةَ ولا القيم. وعاد rent_2026q1 من نوع float64 مع أن الملف حمله int64: فقد وضعته الإذابة في عمود واحد مع أرباعٍ فيها فراغات، والعمود الذي فيه فراغ لا يستطيع أن يكون عددًا صحيحًا. والأخير هو درسُ Int64 في الدرس 3 يصل من طريق جديد، وهو سببُ احتياج equals أعلاه إلى astype(float) حتى توافق.

pivot_table, aggfunc, and the duplicates it averages without telling youpivot_table وaggfunc والمكرَّرات التي تحسب متوسطها دون أن تخبرك

What if two rows claim the same listing and the same quarter? A grid has one cell for them, so something must give. pivot refuses the job; pivot_table accepts it and averages them. Which of those two behaviours you want depends entirely on what you are doing — and the dangerous case is believing you have pivot's guarantee while calling pivot_table.

Which shape a model needs, and which a human readsأي شكل يحتاجه النموذج وأيٌّ يقرؤه الإنسان

The rule is short. Compute in long, present in wide. Every grouping, every join, every time axis and every feature matrix wants one row per observation, because that is what groupby, merge and a model all assume. Every table a human is about to read wants the categories across the top, because an eye compares along a row faster than down a column of labels. So the wide table at the end of an analysis is a presentation step, produced by pivot_table from the long frame you actually computed on — never the thing you compute on.

AI lensمنظور الذكاء الاصطناعي

Shape decides what a model can learn, before any encoding does. In wide form, “the rent in Q3” is a separate feature from “the rent in Q2”, so a model fitted on four quarter columns learns four unrelated weights and cannot be given a fifth quarter at all — the matrix width would change, which is the shape mismatch of Lesson 5 arriving from the time axis instead of the category axis. In long form the same information is one rent column plus one quarter column, the model learns one relationship, and a fifth quarter is 40 more rows rather than a schema change. The same choice is why training data for a language model is one example per row rather than one column per turn. And the seven blank quarters are the leakage trap hiding in here: fill them by carrying the previous quarter forward and you have put a flat's Q3 rent into its Q2 feature, which is leakage across time — the cardinal sin of time-series features, and Lesson 2 already explained why ffill is the wrong instrument on a file whose rows are not a sequence.

The two-second checkالفحص في ثانيتين

Before assigning any Series into a frame, ask what its index is. s.index.equals(df.index) answers it in one line, and an assertion to that effect is cheaper than the bug. The shortcut that never needs the question: if you are assigning the result of a groupby, you wanted transform.

قبل إسناد أي متسلسلة إلى إطار، اسأل ما فهرسُها. وتجيب s.index.equals(df.index) في سطر واحد، والتأكيدُ بذلك أرخص من العطب. والطريقُ المختصر الذي لا يحتاج السؤال قط: إن كنت تُسند ناتجَ groupby فقد أردتَ transform.

transform: one value per original rowtransform: قيمةٌ لكل صف أصلي

The groupby transform computes exactly the same group statistics and then broadcasts each group's value back onto every row of that group, so what comes out is indexed like the frame it came from. One method name, and the assignment becomes legal.

يحسب تحويلُ المجموعات (groupby transform) مقاييسَ المجموعات نفسها بالضبط ثم يبثّ قيمةَ كل مجموعة على كل صف من صفوفها، فيخرج الناتجُ مفهرسًا كالإطار الذي جاء منه. اسمُ دالة واحد، فيصير الإسنادُ مشروعًا.

Beautiful line, wrong place. transform computes the medians from whatever frame you call it on, so calling it on all 480 flats computes each median from the held-out rows as well — textbook leakage. The fitted version computes the five medians on the 360 training rows once, stores them in fitted["area_by_rooms"], and applies them with .map(), which looks up rather than recomputes. That is exactly what Lesson 2's fit_missing does, and why it is a dictionary and not a transform. AI lensمنظور الذكاء الاصطناعي

Group statistics are the most productive feature family in tabular machine learning and the most common source of silent leakage, and both facts come from the same line of code. rent_vs_city above is a strong feature — and if you build it on the whole file before splitting, every held-out flat's own rent has contributed to the median it is being divided by, so the model is being told a little about its own answer. That is the target-encoding leak of Lesson 5 in a new costume, and it has the same nasty property: it flatters the held-out score too, so the measurement you would use to catch it is itself contaminated. The production rule is blunt and worth memorising. A group statistic computed from the target may only ever be fitted on the training rows, and a group statistic that is not from the target may still only be fitted once, because a value that moves between batches makes two months incomparable. The 7 flats above at more than twice their city median are also a labelling opportunity: in a fraud or anomaly system that ratio, not the raw price, is the feature that works.

Honest accounting — what a group feature costsحسابٌ أمين — ما يكلّفه مقياسُ المجموعة خاصيةً

Three costs, all measurable. A group of one carries no information: in the six-row trace above, Jabalia's single listing is its own median, so its ratio is exactly 1.000 and the feature says nothing at all about it. On the 480 flats, five of the twenty agencies appear exactly once (four on the 503 raw rows), so an agency-level statistic is a memorised value for those five rows — the cardinality problem of Lesson 5, met again from the feature side. A group statistic from the target is a fitted parameter and must be stored, which makes fitted bigger and the artefact harder to version. And transform is slower than it looks on a high-cardinality key: it computes the statistic per group and then expands it, so 480 rows over 20 agencies is instant and 10 million rows over 3 million customer ids is not. When the key is nearly unique, a group statistic is a lookup table with extra steps, and Lesson 5's Other bucket is the honest alternative.

Three defences, each one line, and they catch different things. Here they are as the pipeline's actual enrich step, in the (frame, log_row) shape.

Correct on a good day and silent on a bad one. def enrich(d):

    unmatched = int((out["_merge"] == "left_only").sum())

Fixes the first named defect: an unmatched city is now a number you can print and act on, rather than a column of blanks nobody reads. _merge is dropped again straight away, so it costs nothing downstream. def enrich(d):
    n = len(d)
    out = d.merge(REF, on="city", how="left", validate="m:1", indicator=True)
    assert len(out) == n, "the reference join changed the row count: %d -> %d" % (n, len(out))
    unmatched = int((out["_merge"] == "left_only").sum())
    return out.drop(columns="_merge"), unmatched

Fixes the second defect twice over, which is deliberate. validate="m:1" asserts the key is unique on the right and raises a MergeError before the frame exists; the row-count assertion catches the same damage from any other cause, including a future edit that changes how=. تُصلح العيبَ الثاني مرتين، وهذا مقصود. فتؤكّد validate="m:1" أن المفتاح فريد يمينًا وترفع MergeError قبل أن يوجد الإطار؛ ويُسقط تأكيدُ عدد الصفوف الضررَ نفسه من أي سبب آخر، ومنه تعديلٌ لاحق يغيّر how=. والحزامُ والحمّالتان معًا مبرَّران هنا لأن هذه هي العملية الوحيدة في الفصل التي تستطيع تغيير عدد الصفوف في الاتجاهين. وما زال مفتوحًا: فالدالةُ لا تصلح لإعادة الاستعمال مع جدول مرجع ثانٍ، والعمودُ الذي تضيفه غير متماثل عند التكرار.

The recommended form, and three changes earn their place. It returns the (frame, log_row) pair, so the unmatched count lands in the pipeline's report instead of on somebody's screen. And it restores the caller's index, because merge always returns a fresh RangeIndex and Concept 2 has just shown what a changed index does to an assignment.

AI lensمنظور الذكاء الاصطناعي

A fan-out is the most common way a training set quietly acquires the same example many times, and duplicated examples are weights. A model trained on the 644-row frame above has been told that Gaza matters twice as much as the data says, so it will fit Gaza's rents better and everywhere else worse, and no validation score will reveal it — because the held-out split was taken from the same duplicated frame, so the duplicates are on both sides and a flat can appear in training and in testing at once. That last sentence is the serious one: a fan-out before a split is a leak, not just an imbalance, and it is why Lesson 1 insisted that the split comes after stage 1 and Lesson 3 measured what a straddling duplicate costs. The production habits that follow are three: join reference data before splitting and assert the row count, never after; log the row count of every step so a fan-out shows up as a number rather than as a mysterious accuracy change; and treat a one-to-many join (a flat to its four quarters) as a different operation from a reference join, because it is supposed to multiply rows and therefore needs validate="1:m" rather than an assertion that the count did not move.

This concept is about not breaking a table, and it is worth being clear that joining the reference does not, by itself, help the chapter's question at all. Concept 5 measures it: adding km_to_gaza_city to the sixteen-column matrix changes the held-out error from 37.0908 to 37.0908 — not approximately, exactly — because the distance from Gaza City is a property of the city, and the five city dummies already say which city a flat is in. The reference columns are a linear combination of columns the matrix has, which is the dummy-variable trap of Lesson 5 arriving from a join; Appendix C shows the rank deficiency. Drop the city dummies and keep the distance and the error is 46.5113 against 40.6052, so the information is real and it is simply already there. The real fix is the fitted category listالعلاجُ الحقيقي هو قائمة الفئات المضبوطة

Every repair in this concept so far has been an attempt to reconcile two column sets after they diverged. When concat is exactly the right toolحين تكون concat هي الأداة الصحيحة بالضبط

Do not read this concept as “avoid concat”. Stacking is the right operation for the two jobs it was designed for. Combining raw batches before the pipeline runs: pd.concat([march, june], keys=["march", "june"]) on the unencoded files is safe, because both have the same thirteen columns straight from the source, and then one pipeline run transforms both. And assembling a frame column by column: axis=1, which is how Lesson 5's apply_encoder builds its matrix out of a city block, an agency block and five numeric columns. The rule is the same in both: stack things whose columns you control, not things whose columns were inferred.

لا تقرأ هذا المفهوم «تجنّب concat». فالرصُّ هو العملية الصحيحة للمهمّتين اللتين صُمّم لهما. جمعُ الدفعات الخام قبل أن يعمل الخط: فـpd.concat([march, june], keys=["march", "june"]) على الملفين غير المرمَّزين آمنٌ، لأن لكليهما الأعمدةَ الثلاثة عشر نفسها من المصدر مباشرة، ثم يحوّل تشغيلٌ واحد للخط كليهما. وتركيبُ إطار عمودًا عمودًا: بـaxis=1، وهذا كيف تبني apply_encoder في الدرس 5 مصفوفتها من مقطع مدن ومقطع وكالات وخمسة أعمدة عددية. والقاعدةُ واحدة في الحالتين: رُصَّ أشياءَ تملك التحكّم في أعمدتها لا أشياءَ استُنبِطت أعمدتها.

AI lensمنظور الذكاء الاصطناعي

The 54-column frame above is what a feature store looks like when nobody owns the schema, and the three failures it demonstrates are the three that break models in production. A column that exists in training and not at serving time becomes a NaN the model was never fitted on, and most libraries raise on that, which is the good case. A column that exists at serving time and not in training is dropped by any sensible reindex, so the new agency or the new condition level becomes invisible — the model keeps predicting, confidently, about a category it has never been told exists. And a column whose meaning shifted is the one that costs money: join="inner" above produced a city_Gaza column whose zeros mean “not Gaza” for June rows and “not Gaza, or spelled differently, we cannot tell” for March rows. Nothing raises, the shapes agree, and the model is being fed two different features in one column. The defence is the same three lines every time: fit the column list once, store it, and assert that every batch matches it — which is exactly the third assertion of Concept 5.

إطارُ الأعمدة الـ54 أعلاه هو ما يبدو عليه مخزنُ خصائص لا يملك أحدٌ مخطَّطَه، والأعطالُ الثلاثة التي يُظهرها هي الثلاثةُ التي تُعطب النماذج في الإنتاج. فالعمودُ الموجود في التدريب وغيرُ الموجود عند التقديم يصير NaN لم يُضبَط النموذج عليه قط، ومعظمُ المكتبات ترفع خطأً على ذلك، وهذه هي الحالة الجيدة. والعمودُ الموجود عند التقديم وغيرُ الموجود في التدريب تُسقطه أي إعادةِ فهرسة عاقلة، فتصير الوكالةُ الجديدة أو مستوى الحالة الجديد غيرَ مرئي — ويظلّ النموذج يتنبّأ بثقة عن فئة لم يُخبَر بوجودها قط. والعمودُ الذي انزاح معناه هو الذي يكلّف مالًا: فقد أنتج join="inner" أعلاه عمودَ city_Gaza أصفارُه تعني «ليست غزة» لصفوف حزيران و«ليست غزة، أو مكتوبةٌ بصورة أخرى، لا نستطيع أن نقول» لصفوف آذار. ولا شيء يرفع خطأً، والأشكالُ متوافقة، والنموذجُ يُغذّى بخاصيتين مختلفتين في عمود واحد. والدفاعُ هو الأسطرُ الثلاثة نفسها في كل مرة: اضبط قائمةَ الأعمدة مرةً واحدة واحفظها وأكّد أن كل دفعة تطابقها — وهذا بالضبط التأكيدُ الثالث في المفهوم 5.

Why is this worse than the 54-column version with 4,491 blanks?يُرمَّز آذار وحزيران على انفراد ويُرَصّان بـjoin="inner". والناتجُ 588 صفًّا و11 عمودًا وبلا خلايا فارغة. فلماذا هذا أسوأ من نسخة الأعمدة الـ54 التي فيها 4491 فراغًا؟

A blank is information: it says “this column was not defined for this batch”, and isna().sum() finds all 4,491 of them in one line. An all-zero row says nothing at all, and after an inner join the 187 March rows whose city was misspelled are indistinguishable from the two genuine Beit Lahia listings, 189 all-zero rows in all. The first option is a reasonable-sounding distraction: eleven columns is in fact the correct width for this data and the fitted version uses five for the city. The third is a factual error worth being sure about — join= controls which columns survive, never which rows; a concat on axis=0 keeps every row of every frame, always. The fourth is true and is the mildest of the four consequences: it is a signal, not the damage.

def transform_listings(df, fitted=None):
    """The whole canonical order. fitted=None: learn from THESE rows, return the parameters.

    Returns (matrix, report, fitted). The matrix keeps the input's index, so the
    target lines up with df.loc[X.index, TARGET] and never travels inside X.
    """
    log = []
    frame, l12 = make_comparable(df)                   # stages 1 and 2: no parameters
    log += l12
    for step in ENRICH:                                # the reference join, asserted
        frame, row = step(frame, fitted)
        log.append(row)
    n_rows = len(frame)                                # the row count stage 1 settled
    if fitted is None:
        fitted = {}
        fitted.update(fit_missing(frame))
        fitted.update(fit_outliers(frame))
        fitted.update(fit_encoder(frame))
        fitted["fitted_on"] = n_rows
    for step in STAGE_3 + STAGE_4:
        frame, row = step(frame, fitted)
        log.append(row)
    X, row = s5_encode(frame, fitted)
    log.append(row)
    fitted.setdefault("columns", list(X.columns))

    # --- three claims this function will not return without ---
    assert len(X) == n_rows, "row count changed after stage 1: %d -> %d" % (n_rows, len(X))
    blank = X.columns[X.isna().any()].tolist()
    assert not blank, "blank cells in the feature matrix: %s" % blank
    assert list(X.columns) == fitted["columns"], "column set differs from the fitted one"

    return X, pd.DataFrame(log), fitted

The recommended form. Three lines are new and each one is a sentence the function now refuses to break. The row count has not moved since stage 1 — which catches a fan-out, a stray dropna and a merge somebody changed to how="inner". No column of the matrix contains a blank — which catches a fill whose fallback was removed and a new column added without one. The column set is exactly the fitted one, in order — which catches a stale artefact, a renamed column and the two-column-sets problem of Concept 4. They cost microseconds, they run on every batch for ever, and the difference between an assertion and a comment is that a comment cannot stop a bad frame reaching a model. Three subsections from now you will see all three fire.

On a held-out median rent of about 195 shekels, an error of 36.96 is roughly 19% per listing. For a housing officer deciding whether an asking price is reasonable that is useful; for anybody who needs the rent of this flat to within a few shekels it is not, and no amount of transformation in this chapter closes that gap. Three caveats travel with the number and all three matter. It is one split with one seed, and Lesson 1 measured the noise on this ruler at a shekel or two, so 36.9553 against 37.0908 is not a difference. The model is a straight line, which is the simplest thing that can use these columns and certainly not the best. The three things transform_listings returns are exactly the three things a machine-learning system needs from its data layer, and naming them is worth more than any one technique in this chapter. The matrix is what the model consumes, and its contract is a fixed column order — which is why the third assertion exists, because a permuted matrix does not raise and predicts confident nonsense. The report is the monitoring: feature statistics per batch, compared with the previous batch, is how drift is detected weeks before accuracy moves. Put March's and June's reports side by side and you can already see three months of signal — %d/%m/%Y=23 becoming =0, unmatched gaining a value, unseen naming a city — and none of those would appear in a model-quality metric until the damage was done. The fitted dictionary is the model artefact: ship it with the weights or the service receives raw metres and five city strings and has no idea what column 11 was. The failure this prevents is called training–serving skew, it is the most common way a model that validated beautifully performs badly in production, and almost every instance of it is one of the three assertions above that nobody wrote.

Why does transform_listings take fitted=None as a default, rather than having two separate functions fit_listings and apply_listings?لماذا تأخذ transform_listings قيمة fitted=None افتراضيةً بدل أن تكون دالتين منفصلتين fit_listings وapply_listings؟

Everything in this lesson is in service of one guarantee: June goes through the same transformations as March. Two functions make that a thing you have to maintain by hand, and the day somebody adds a step to fit_listings and forgets apply_listings is the day training–serving skew enters the system, silently, with no failing test. import json

def save_fitted(fitted, path, source, rows):
    """A parameter file is only useful if it says when, from what, and with which library."""
    def plain(v):
        if isinstance(v, dict):   return {("rooms=%g" % k if isinstance(k, float) else str(k)): plain(x)
                                          for k, x in v.items()}
        if isinstance(v, (list, tuple)): return [plain(x) for x in v]
        if isinstance(v, (np.integer,)): return int(v)
        if isinstance(v, (np.floating,)): return float(v)
        return v
    artefact = {"fitted_at": "2026-06-30", "source": source, "rows_fitted_on": rows,
                "pandas": pd.__version__, "columns": fitted["columns"],
                "params": {k: plain(v) for k, v in fitted.items() if k != "columns"}}
    text = json.dumps(artefact, indent=1, sort_keys=True, ensure_ascii=False)
    open(path, "w", encoding="utf-8").write(text)
    return text

There is a second thing nothing writes down, and it is subtler: the row order of the output is not reproducible, and it decides the training split. s1_one_row_per_flat sorts by listed to choose which copy of a repost survives, and sort_values defaults to a non-stable sort.

The content is idempotent — run the pipeline on its own output and you get the same 480 flats with the same values, which is the property Concept 3's Version 4 worked for. The order is not: 215 of 480 positions move. On its own that is harmless, and then the next line is not harmless at all: sample() selects by position, so a different row order draws a different training set — 82 of the 360 training rows swap for other rows. Which means the one-word fix, kind="stable", cannot be applied quietly: it would redraw the split and move every measured number in Lessons 4, 5 and 6 by a shekel or two. One split, one seed, no cross-validationتقسيمٌ واحد وبذرةٌ واحدة وبلا تحقّق متقاطع

Every number in Concept 5's table comes from one 75/25 split with random_state=3. That is one draw from a space of enormously many, and 113 held-out rents is a small sample: the 36.9553 has a standard error of a shekel or two all by itself, which Lesson 1 measured and Lesson 5 had to run ten seeds to see past. Three honest consequences. Differences under about two shekels in that table mean nothing, which is why the recommendation is “drop the agency block” on the grounds that it is free, not on the grounds that 36.9553 beats 37.0908. The 36.9553 itself is an estimate of an estimate, and quoting it to four decimal places — as this lesson does, because reproducibility requires it — is not the same as believing four decimal places. And a single split cannot tell you whether a step helps reliably, only whether it helped once. The instrument that answers this is k-fold cross-validation: split the rows into k parts, fit on k−1 and measure on the remaining one, k times, and report the spread as well as the mean. It is not in this chapter, it is not hard, and every claim here would be stronger with it.

The feature that improves both columns and must not be shippedالخاصيةُ التي تُحسّن العمودين ولا يجوز إرسالها

The frame has a deposit column, it correlates 0.6034 with the rent on the recorded pairs, and adding it to the ten-column matrix improves the training error from 37.1710 to 33.0754 and the held-out error from 36.9553 to 35.6282. For 87 of the 360 training rows — 24.2% — the deposit is a multiple of the rent, because Lesson 2's rule computed it as months × monthly_rent. A quarter of that column is the answer, arithmetically transformed. It is the target-encoding leak of Lesson 5 in its purest form, and it has the same vicious property: because the leak is inside each row's own feature, it flatters the held-out score too, so the measurement you would use to catch it has been contaminated by it. In production the deposit would be unknown at the moment you need the rent prediction, which is the test that settles it: a feature you will not have when you need the prediction is not a feature, however well it measures.

A key that is unique on one side cannot fan out, because every term is n × 1 = n — this is exactly the claim validate="m:1" enforces. A fan-out is multiplicative, not additive, so it grows alarmingly: 40 listings with four history rows each, merged with themselves, give 40 × 4² = 640, and the same join on a key with ten rows per side would give 100 per key. And a left join does not protect you, because how= decides what happens to a term whose right count is zero (left keeps it as one blank row, inner drops it) and says nothing at all about a term whose right count is two. A least-squares fit on a rank-deficient matrix does not fail — np.linalg.lstsq returns the minimum-norm solution, spreading the weight across the dependent columns — and it cannot improve the fit, because the predictions live in the same column space either way. This is the dummy-variable trap of Lesson 5, arriving from a join rather than from an encoder, and the full matrix shows it three times over: rank 16 of 19, because the city block, the agency block and km_to_gaza are each a combination of the others plus the intercept.

Two practical consequences. A reference join can add zero information and it is cheap to find out: if a reference column is constant within the key you joined on, and that key is already one-hot encoded, the column is redundant by construction. And redundancy is not harmless: a rank-deficient matrix gives coefficients that are unstable and uninterpretable (any amount can be shuffled between the dependent columns), so a model you wanted to read as well as use is worse off. Where the join does earn its place is in the two things one-hot encoding cannot do: it describes a city the training data has never seen — Beit Lahia has a km value and no dummy — and it lets you say “rent falls with distance from the centre”, which is a sentence about a market rather than five unrelated weights.

Key takeaways — the lesson, and the chapterخلاصات — الدرس والفصل

This lesson, in six sentences. Compute in long and present in wide, and remember that melt turns blank cells into rows rather than dropping them. agg gives one row per group and transform one value per row, and if you are assigning the result into a frame you wanted transform. A merge is the only operation here that can both delete and duplicate rows, so clean the key, pass validate=, and assert the row count. concat aligns columns by name, so two independently encoded batches cannot be stacked safely and the fix is the fitted category list, not join="inner". A pipeline is one function with a fitted parameter, a per-batch report and assertions it refuses to return without. And a pipeline whose author can list four things wrong with it is more trustworthy than one whose author cannot.

The chapter, in one page. Six lessons took 503 scraped rows to a 480 × 18 matrix and the held-out error of the question “what rent should this flat ask?” from 63.67 shekels to 36.96. The order was fixed first (Lesson 1): structure, values, missing, outliers, encode and scale, with every parameter fitted on the training rows alone. Then the holes were decided by mechanism rather than habit (Lesson 2): 19 rows dropped, not 174, because only the target may not be filled. Then values were made comparable (Lesson 3): one row per flat, 26 spellings to 5, twelve booleans to two, nineteen ground floors kept, two date formats parsed behind two assertions. Then thresholds were given rules (Lesson 4): a LIMITS dictionary, a fitted robust z, a cross-column ratio check, every finding a flag with a count and never a deletion. Then words became numbers (Lesson 5): fitted category lists, a declared level order, three scalers, and a matrix whose columns do not move when the file changes. And this lesson put them in one function that reports what it did, stores what it learned, and refuses to return a frame that breaks its own contract.

The mistakes cheat-sheet. Assigning a groupby result instead of a transform (a column of NaN, or worse, a few right by luck). Merging on a key you have not cleaned (316 of 503). Trusting how="left" to protect the row count (644 from 480). Stacking two separately encoded batches (4,491 blanks) and then “fixing” it with join="inner" (189 indistinguishable all-zero rows). Letting pivot_table average duplicates with its default aggfunc (a cell that belongs to no flat). Computing a group fill with transform on the whole file (leakage). Keeping a feature that will not exist when you need the prediction (the deposit, which improves both error columns and is a quarter made of the answer). Refitting a parameter on the new month (two incomparable months). And editing a module constant that a stored artefact should own (75 flats renumbered, silently, with every assertion passing).

What is honestly still open. fitted is a dict in memory unless you write Exercise 5; six limits, a 1.5 and a 3.5 are reviewed judgements rather than derived numbers, and dep_months_all is a median that flips between 1 and 2 depending on the sample; every measurement is one split with one seed; the row order is not reproducible until the sort is made stable; and feature engineering has not started — eighteen columns, not one of them invented by somebody who understands the market.

الفصلُ في صفحة. أخذت ستةُ دروس 503 صفوف مزحوفة إلى مصفوفة 480 × 18، وأخذت خطأَ الصفوف المحجوزة لسؤال «كم ينبغي أن تطلب هذه الشقة إيجارًا؟» من 63.67 شيكلًا إلى 36.96. ثُبّت الترتيبُ أولًا (الدرس 1): البنيةُ ثم القيمُ ثم المفقودُ ثم الشواذُّ ثم الترميزُ وتوحيدُ المقياس، وكلُّ وسيط مضبوطٌ على صفوف التدريب وحدها. ثم قُرّرت الثقوبُ بالآلية لا بالعادة (الدرس 2): ‏19 صفًّا محذوفًا لا 174، لأن الهدفَ وحده لا يجوز تعويضه. ثم جُعلت القيمُ قابلةً للمقارنة (الدرس 3): صفٌّ لكل شقة، و26 صورة إملائية إلى 5، واثنتا عشرة صورة منطقية إلى اثنتين، وتسعةَ عشر طابقًا أرضيًّا محفوظة، وصيغتا تاريخ محلَّلتان وراء تأكيدين. ثم أُعطيت الحدودُ قواعد (الدرس 4): قاموسُ LIMITS وz حصينةٌ مضبوطة وفحصُ نسبة عابرٌ للأعمدة، وكلُّ كشفٍ علمٌ بعدد لا حذفٌ قط. ثم صارت الكلماتُ أرقامًا (الدرس 5): قوائمُ فئات مضبوطة وترتيبُ مستويات معلَن وثلاثةُ مقايِس ومصفوفةٌ لا تتحرّك أعمدتها حين يتغيّر الملف. وهذا الدرسُ وضعها في دالة واحدة تُبلّغ عمّا فعلته وتحفظ ما تعلّمته وترفض أن تُرجع إطارًا يخالف عقدَها.

ورقةُ الأخطاء. إسنادُ ناتج groupby بدل transform (عمودٌ من NaN، أو أسوأ: قلةٌ صحيحة بالحظ). والدمجُ على مفتاح لم تنظّفه (‏316 من 503). والثقةُ بأن how="left" تحمي عددَ الصفوف (‏644 من 480). ورصُّ دفعتين مرمَّزتين على انفراد (‏4491 فراغًا) ثم «إصلاحُه» بـjoin="inner" (‏189 صفًّا صفريًّا لا يُميَّز). وتركُ pivot_table تحسب متوسطَ المكرَّرات بـaggfunc الافتراضية (خليةٌ لا تخصّ أي شقة). وحسابُ تعويض مجموعة بـtransform على الملف كله (تسرّب). والإبقاءُ على خاصية لن توجد حين تحتاج التنبّؤ (التأمين، وهو يُحسّن عمودَي الخطأ ورُبعُه مصنوعٌ من الجواب). وإعادةُ ضبط وسيط على الشهر الجديد (شهران غير قابلين للمقارنة). وتحريرُ ثابت وحدة ينبغي أن يملكه أثرٌ محفوظ (‏75 شقة أُعيد ترقيمها بصمت وكلُّ تأكيد ناجح).

وما يبقى مفتوحًا بأمانة. fitted قاموسٌ في الذاكرة إلا أن تكتب التمرين 5؛ وستةُ حدود و1.5 و3.5 أحكامٌ مُراجَعة لا أرقامٌ مستنبَطة، وdep_months_all وسيطٌ ينقلب بين 1 و2 تبعًا للعيّنة؛ وكلُّ قياس تقسيمٌ واحد ببذرة واحدة؛ وترتيبُ الصفوف غيرُ قابل للتكرار حتى يُجعَل الفرز مستقرًّا؛ وهندسةُ الخصائص لم تبدأ — ثمانيةَ عشر عمودًا ولا واحد منها اختلقه من يفهم السوق.
