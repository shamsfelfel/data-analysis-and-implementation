# Shared sample datasets

Generated by `python tools/make_datasets.py` (deterministic). Lessons link to the files under
`downloads/` with `href="@/downloads/<chapter>/<file>"` and quote the numbers below — never
invent a different number for the same data. Re-run the script, never hand-edit the files.

## Facts (computed from the files)

- T1 rental_listings.csv: 60 rows; 4 blank area_m2, 2 blank monthly_rent, 1 blank rooms; columns listing_id, city, governorate, monthly_rent, area_m2, rooms, furnished, listed_on, advert
- T1 rental_listings_ar_semicolon.csv: 30 rows, ';' delimiter, decimal comma in المساحة_م2, UTF-8 with BOM
- T1 rental_listings.xlsx: sheet 'Raw' has 3 title rows above the header at row 4 and listed_on as serial numbers (General format); sheet 'Clean' has real dates. 40 rows. 
- T1 schools: 13 schools in 3 API pages of 5 (schools_api_page1..3.json) and one document schools.json; contacts missing on 3 schools, email present on 3
- P1 cafeteria_orders.csv: 771 rows (lines), columns order_id, order_time, item, category, qty, unit_price, payment, customer_type; period 2026-09-01..2026-09-30, closed Fri/Sat
- P1 mess: 16 exactly-duplicated lines; 17 blank payment; 8 item spelling variants (case/space of Latte, Tea, Espresso); 1 price typo (Shawarma Wrap at 120.0); 1 unit_price 'n/a' (pandas read_csv reads 'n/a' as missing BY DEFAULT, so there the column loads as float64 with one NaN; Excel and Power BI see it as text/error); 5 refunds with qty = -1
- P1 distinct order_id: 616; distinct items in raw: 22; distinct items after strip+title: 16
- P1 after dropping duplicates, refunds, the typo and 'n/a': 748 lines; total revenue = 8062.00
- P1 top 3 items by revenue: Shawarma Wrap=1368.00; Chicken Rice=1020.00; Falafel Sandwich=917.00
- P1 revenue by category: Food=4205.00; Drink=2769.00; Snack=747.00; Dessert=341.00
- P1 busiest hour by distinct orders: 12:00 (119 orders); then 13:00 (117), 11:00 (99)
- T2 listings_raw.csv: 503 rows, columns listing_id, city, governorate, agency, monthly_rent, area_m2, rooms, floor, condition, furnished, deposit, listed_on, advert; listed 2026-02-01..2026-06-19 - the window is four months but the last date runs past it, because one repost (listing_id 5254) carries a later listed_on than the batch it repeats
- T2 duplicates come in two kinds: 14 rows are byte-for-byte repeats (df.duplicated().sum()) and 9 more are reposts - the same listing_id with a later listed_on and the advert retyped, so every column does not match and drop_duplicates() cannot see them. drop_duplicates() leaves 489 rows; drop_duplicates(subset=['listing_id']) leaves 480, which is the number of real flats
- T2 city spellings: 26 distinct strings in the raw column for 5 real cities; lowercasing and collapsing spaces still leaves 11 (Khan Yunis / Khan Younis, Jabalya / Jabalia differ by more than case) - a canonical map is needed, not .str.title()
- T2 furnished is written 12 ways: ' no', '0', '1', 'FALSE', 'N', 'No', 'TRUE', 'Y', 'Yes', 'no', 'yes', 'yes '
- T2 floor is a mixed-type column: 19 rows hold 'G' for ground and 9 are blank, so it loads as text and to_numeric(errors='coerce') turns every 'G' into NaN - losing the fact that a ground floor IS a floor
- T2 missing (blank cells): monthly_rent=19, area_m2=27, floor=9, condition=36, deposit=123
- T2 the agency predicts WHETHER the deposit is blank, not WHAT it would have been - keep those two claims apart. Within-agency spread is 201.8 (median across agencies) against 206.5 over the whole file, so grouping by agency removes about 2% of the spread; and the deposit correlates 0.6034 with the rent against 0.1375 with its own agency's mean. A per-agency median is therefore NOT a better fill than a global one - measure it, do not assume it
- T2 missingness is NOT one mechanism, which is the whole point of the column. area_m2 blanks are spread at random (MCAR). deposit is blank for every row of Noor Estates and Diwan Rentals plus a few others: it depends on `agency`, a column you can see (MAR), which identifies the mechanism. monthly_rent is blank where the rent is high - the site prints 'price on request' for premium flats (MNAR): mean rooms 3.58 vs 3.04, mean area 81.5 vs 71.8 m2, 32% excellent vs 15%, 42% furnished vs 33%. Those are hints, not proof - MNAR cannot be proved from the file, only learned from the source - and imputing the mean rent drags exactly the most expensive listings down to average
- T2 monthly_rent (the 484 rows that have one): min=25, median=200.0, mean=416.3, max=99999, std=4536.6 - mean above median, so right-skewed
- T2 IQR fence on monthly_rent: Q1=153.8, Q3=255.0, IQR=101.2, fences [1.9, 406.9]; 12 values fall outside (0 low, 12 high). |z|>3 flags only 1 of them, because the single 99999 inflates the standard deviation it is measured against - drop that one typo and |z|>3 flags 6 while IQR flags 11. Note what NEITHER rule finds: the daily rate of 25 sits inside the lower fence of 1.9, so only domain knowledge catches it
- T2 planted value errors, which are NOT the same thing as outliers: monthly_rent 99999 (listing_id 5062, a phone number) and 25 (listing_id 5208, a daily rate); rooms 0 (listing_id 5134) and 12 (listing_id 5319); 5 area_m2 values recorded in dunum, so they read 0.0x instead of 5x-9x m2 (5015, 5099, 5167, 5290, 5403)
- T2 the cross-column check that beats every single-column rule: deposit / monthly_rent is exactly 1, 2 or 3 months in 378 of the 380 recorded pairs, and the 2 exceptions are precisely the two rent errors (listing 5208 ratio 5.4000; listing 5062 ratio 0.0015). One ratio check therefore finds the daily rate of 25, which no statistic on the rent column alone can reach - it sits inside the IQR fence and its robust z is only -2.36
- T2 the fence changes its mind on a log scale: 1.5*IQR on log1p(monthly_rent) gives fences of 71.71 and 543.78 shekels and flags 8 values - 5 low and 3 high - where the fence on the raw column flags 12, all of them high. The 25 is caught only here, because taking logs is what makes the lower fence bite
- T2 condition is ordinal (poor < fair < good < excellent) and arrives in 14 non-blank spellings plus 36 blanks; after strip+lower: good=188, fair=144, excellent=79, poor=56, (blank)=36
- T2 agency is high-cardinality: 20 agencies over 503 rows; the top 5 carry 364 rows (72%) while 4 agencies appear exactly once and 8 appear at most three times - one-hot encoding it adds 20 columns, most of them almost all zeros
- T2 listed_on: 480 rows ISO (YYYY-MM-DD) and 23 rows DD/MM/YYYY; pd.to_datetime without format= warns and falls back per value, so 03/04/2026 and 2026-04-03 can end up as different days
- T2 the merge trap: city_reference.csv holds the 5 cities under their canonical spelling plus Beit Lahia (in no listing). Merging on the RAW city column matches 316 of 503 rows (63%) and an inner join silently drops the rest; merging on the cleaned name matches all 503. An outer join on the clean name leaves Beit Lahia as 1 unmatched reference row
- T2 listings_new.csv: 85 rows for June 2026, already canonically spelled, and carrying what the training data never saw - city: Beit Lahia; agencies: Andalus Homes, Burhan Realty, Sumud Housing; condition: very good. Plus 3 blank area_m2, 2 blank monthly_rent, 1 blank condition. get_dummies() on this file alone produces different columns than on listings_raw.csv, which is the whole point
- T2 rent_history_wide.csv: 40 listings x 4 quarter columns; melting gives 160 long rows, of which 153 carry a rent and 7 are blank quarters
